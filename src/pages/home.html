Why AfroBot

Talent Is Everywhere. Opportunity Isn’t.

Young people are already curious. They take things apart, ask what-if, and help one another. What is not evenly shared is a bench to build on, a mentor who can stay, a competition that will have them, and a next step after a workshop ends.

AfroBot is for communities where robotics and AI have been too far, too expensive, or too closed. This page does not invent a statistic to describe that gap. When a way in is actually open, we will say so.

Cost

World-Class Opportunities. Community-Friendly Costs.

A family should not have to buy a whole lab before a child can make something. AfroBot is designed so cost is not the thing that decides who starts.

  • Shared equipment

    Kits and tools stay with the program instead of becoming a purchase for every child.

  • Sponsored places

    When funding exists, a sponsor can cover a place. Those places are not listed yet.

  • Volunteer mentors

    Adults give time. Anyone who will be with students is screened first.

  • Grants

    A host site may be supported by a grant. No grant is announced on this site today.

Goal, not a price

Our goal is an entry workshop around $10. That amount is a goal, not a current price. Nothing on this page is an offer to register, and no fee is being charged from this website.

Ask about financial assistance

This inquiry does not ask for financial documents. Please do not send pay stubs, tax returns, benefit letters, or account numbers.

Competitions

From Our Community to the Competition Stage.

Students who want to test their work against a real challenge should be able to prepare for competitions that already exist. AfroBot aims to prepare students for established competitions such as FIRST Tech Challenge.

AfroBot is not affiliated with FIRST. FIRST sets its own rules, fees, and eligibility. The interest list is not team placement. It does not hold a roster spot, a robot, or a competition entry.

After the build

The Journey Doesn’t End With a Robot.

A finished robot is one result. Students also practice teamwork, design, and explaining their work. Some will want another season. Some will want a class, a lab, or a campus conversation when those chances exist.

AfroBot helps students build more than robots. We help them build futures.

That line is a direction, not a promise. AfroBot does not guarantee college admission, scholarships, jobs, or awards.

How we will know

Success Is Measured in Opportunity.

When programs are open, success should be visible without putting children on display. We will explain the method before we publish a figure. There are no results to show yet. This section has no counts and no percentages.

  • Whether a family can find a real starting point.
  • Whether a student who wants to continue has a next step.
  • Whether equipment and mentor time reach communities with less access.
  • Whether students can prepare for competitions whose eligibility they meet.
  • Whether older students can see a path toward further learning.
  • Whether sponsors and volunteers can tell where support went.

We will not publish children’s profiles, rankings, or private family information.
