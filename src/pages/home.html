The problem

A shorter school week in Bamenda. A first season still ahead in Texas.

Cameroon

Kids in Bamenda were in school about four days a week because of the conflict there. Kids in other parts of the country were in school five days a week. Classes began in {{facts.cameroonStarted}}, under Glappy Foundation, so those children could keep learning to build.

Dallas–Fort Worth

In 2024, about {{facts.mckinneyStudents}} children in McKinney, Texas, built in five to six teams, with parents in the room.

Robotics and AI should not depend on a family’s income or zip code.

The model

Learn, build, and show the work.

  1. Learn

    Classes with a mentor. A child takes part through a parent or guardian. Mentors are screened before they meet students.

  2. Build

    Ages 6–12 start with a hands-on build. Ages 13–17 work toward a humanoid robot. In 2027, Dallas–Fort Worth teams build small humanoid robots from open-source schematics and 3D-printed parts.

  3. Show

    Training, safety, and a showcase. Dallas–Fort Worth and Yaoundé meet on separate dates. AfroBot is hosting the Yaoundé event.
