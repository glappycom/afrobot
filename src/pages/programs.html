Programs

Five names for sessions we are preparing.

Discover, Build, Innovate, Compete, and Advance are ways to talk about the work. Ages below are guidance so a family can see where a child might begin. They overlap, and they are not a test.

Not open yet. There is no schedule, no price, and no registration for any of these. If you want to hear when a session is real, get session updates. That list is not a seat in a class and not team placement.

Access

Access is the way in: a workshop, a school, or another host in the community. It is not a sixth class with its own age rule. It is how a family finds Discover, Build, or whatever fits.

Cost is part of access. Shared equipment, sponsored places, volunteer mentors, and grants are how AfroBot plans to keep a start within reach. Our goal is an entry workshop around $10. That amount is a goal, not a current price.

Ask about financial assistance. Please do not send financial documents.

Suggested ages are guidance. They overlap. They are not a test. Where a competition sets eligibility, that rule wins.

Discover

Suggested ages 5–8. Playful building and simple machines, with an adult close by.

Build

Suggested ages 9–12. A longer project: design something, make it, and test it with a team.

Innovate

Suggested ages 12–15. Harder problems, including how AI tools are used and how a team explains its choices.

Compete

Eligibility set by the competition. AfroBot aims to prepare students for established competitions such as FIRST Tech Challenge. AfroBot is not affiliated with FIRST. Joining the interest list is not team placement.

Competition stance

Advance

Suggested ages 15–18. Practice and guidance toward further learning. AfroBot does not guarantee college admission, scholarships, or credit.
